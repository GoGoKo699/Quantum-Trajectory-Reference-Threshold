## Exact localization of faulty devices

Llorens, Diebra, Sedlák, and Muñoz-Tapia formulate zero-error identification of a known number of faulty devices that all apply the same unknown unitary. They optimize success averaged over a Haar-distributed relative unitary and study both parallel and sequential testers. Their independent parallel tester prepares one maximally entangled pair per device, sends one half through that device, and projects the pair onto its original state or the orthogonal subspace. The orthogonal outcome certifies an anomaly. The tester reports the subset when all $`k`$ anomalies are certified. The pointwise probability before the Haar average in Appendix B, Eqs. (B1) and (B4)–(B6), is $`[1-|\mathrm{tr}(U)/d|^2]^k`$. At $`d=2`$ and $`U=\exp(-i\theta Z/2)`$ this gives $`\sin^{2k}(\theta/2)`$. [Llorens et al., *Exact identification of unknown unitary processes*, arXiv:2605.04981v1 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04981v1#page=14).

In the phase-rotation model, prepare each pair in $`(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)/\sqrt 2`$ and measure its symmetric and antisymmetric components. This implements the same tester in the one-excitation sector of each pair. It uses $`N`$ bypass qubits for $`N`$ queried sensors and provides the unit-gain benchmark at every angle, with a wrong-label probability of zero. The resource question here is how much of this conclusive probability a collective probe and receiver can achieve with $`r`$ bypass qubits, each able to store zero or one excitation during interrogation.

## Trajectory sensing and discrete detector networks

Chin, Leibrandt, and Chuang study the same local $`Z`$-rotation model and include all subsets of a fixed size in their symmetric trajectory family. Their perfect discrimination threshold asks whether a probe can make all trajectory outputs orthogonal at a given interaction strength. Their supplemental protocol explicitly takes the interaction strength as input. In the Gaussian-profile example the figure of merit is the probability of misidentifying the closest modeled trajectory. Chin and Chuang use permutation and Pauli symmetries to reduce the fixed-angle orthogonality equations. Here the probe and readout are fixed across a nonempty open interval of angles, and a run may abstain while every conclusive label is correct. The angle-uniform zero conditions eliminate all lower-degree Pauli moments before the finite-width optimization. [Chin, Leibrandt, and Chuang, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **134**, 210802 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05888) and [Chin and Chuang, *Phys. Rev. A* **111**, 052447 (2025), Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00893).

Ali and Hillery use reciprocal states for unambiguous detection with abstention in a discrete-detector network. In Sec. II B, the reciprocal states are defined from a specified unitary, with one local reciprocal vector depending on its angle. Agarwal, Ali, and Hillery minimize wrong guesses for translated patterns in one- and two-dimensional arrays in the weak-interaction regime. The receiver here must give zero wrong reports for every $`k`$-subset at every angle in the promised interval. [Ali and Hillery, *Phys. Rev. A* **110**, 012619 (2024), Sec. II B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20435) and [Agarwal, Ali, and Hillery, *Detection of patterns in a discrete-outcome sensor network*, arXiv:2606.25100 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25100).

Reciprocal measurements and semidefinite optimization of unambiguous discrimination for a fixed pure-state ensemble are established tools. The Gram criterion here uses the degree-$`k`$ moment vectors together with every lower-degree moment that the same receiver must annihilate, and optimizes the input distribution over the excitation weights accessible at a given bypass width. [Eldar, *IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory* **49**, 446–456 (2003)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0206093).

## Number conservation and finite references

Bartlett, Rudolph, and Spekkens describe the global $`U(1)`$ twirl and the resulting separation into total-number sectors. Bartlett, Rudolph, Spekkens, and Turner study a separately prepared finite phase token for transmitting a quantum state between parties without a shared frame. Their phase token occupies a truncated number spectrum and supports relational decoding. The present model uses number-conserving controls and prepares the bypass jointly with the sensors in a fixed total-excitation sector. It supplies compensating charge for relative coherences between sensor weights. Each bypass qubit contributes at most one unit to the sensor charge range, and the receiver reports a classical subset. [Bartlett, Rudolph, and Spekkens, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **79**, 555–609 (2007), Secs. II B and IV A](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610030) and [Bartlett et al., *New J. Phys.* **11**, 063013 (2009), Sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.5040).

The bypass-width frontier has exact onset at $`r=k`$, where the optimal gain relative to $`\sin^{2k}(\theta/2)`$ is $`1/\binom{N}{k}`$. For one hit the gain is $`\min\{1,r^2/N\}`$. For fixed $`k`$, the finite-width Gram criterion characterizes the optimum and a collective construction reaches unit gain with $`O_k(\sqrt N)`$ bypass qubits. This saving measures qubits held outside the sensing interaction. The charge-conserving preparation and readout may require collective gates.
