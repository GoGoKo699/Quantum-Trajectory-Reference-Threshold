# Project understanding map

The map has **12 background milestones** and **18 research milestones**. The first phase connects excitation conservation, angle uncertainty and conclusive measurement. The second phase follows their consequences from a single vanishing amplitude to the full reference-qubit tradeoff and its implementation.

Use the [reference-frames bridge](REFERENCE_FRAMES_BRIDGE.md) for the educational anchor, notation and worked example. The linked scientific pages give the exact statements and proofs.

## Phase I — Background and preliminaries

### M01 Sensor occupation and phase

**Central question.** What changes when a Z rotation acts on a sensor?

**Understanding target.** Explain why a phase gate changes the relative amplitude of the occupied and empty components while preserving their occupation probabilities.

**Prerequisites.** Start here.

**Conceptual connection.** The affected site enters the measurement problem through a phase conditional on its occupation.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md) · [Reference-frames bridge](REFERENCE_FRAMES_BRIDGE.md)

### M02 One interrogation and many possible subsets

**Central question.** What does one event tell the sensors, and what information does the receiver start with?

**Understanding target.** Specify one parallel interrogation of all sensors, a known number of affected sites, an unknown affected subset and angle, and the identity operation on each unaffected site.

**Prerequisites.** [M01](#m01-sensor-occupation-and-phase).

**Conceptual connection.** The hypotheses are the subsets of the known size. Probe preparation and readout are chosen before the event.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md)

### M03 Conclusive identification and abstention

**Central question.** How can a receiver be certain whenever it reports a subset?

**Understanding target.** Distinguish the probability of a wrong label from the probability of an inconclusive result. Explain the pointwise zero-error requirement over subsets and over an open angle interval.

**Prerequisites.** [M02](#m02-one-interrogation-and-many-possible-subsets).

**Conceptual connection.** Because an identity event reveals no site, abstention makes the task possible without requiring success at every angle.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md) · [Reference-frames bridge](REFERENCE_FRAMES_BRIDGE.md)

### M04 The common phase standard

**Central question.** Which angle is unavailable to the laboratory, and which angle is the signal?

**Understanding target.** Explain the phase transformation generated by total excitation. Separate its common phase-coordinate parameter from the event angle that rotates only the affected sites.

**Prerequisites.** [M01](#m01-sensor-occupation-and-phase), [M02](#m02-one-interrogation-and-many-possible-subsets).

**Conceptual connection.** The common transformation shifts all registers together. A local signal can still change relational phases.

**Canonical sources.** [Reference-frames bridge](REFERENCE_FRAMES_BRIDGE.md) · [Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610030v3) (§II B)

### M05 Phase averaging selects number sectors

**Central question.** What information survives averaging over the common phase?

**Understanding target.** Derive the loss of off-diagonal matrix elements between different total excitation values by averaging the appropriate phase factors. Explain why within-sector coherences survive.

**Prerequisites.** [M04](#m04-the-common-phase-standard).

**Conceptual connection.** Both the prepared probe and the final effects can be treated sector by sector, a reduction used by every converse.

**Canonical sources.** [Reference-frames bridge](REFERENCE_FRAMES_BRIDGE.md) · [Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610030v3) (§§II B–C) · [Operational model](../science/model.md)

### M06 One excitation on three rails

**Central question.** How can an idle qubit preserve a usable sensing phase?

**Understanding target.** Trace the bridge's three-basis-state probe through a rotation on either sensor. Identify the untouched amplitude on the idle rail and the component whose phase changes.

**Prerequisites.** [M01](#m01-sensor-occupation-and-phase), [M04](#m04-the-common-phase-standard), [M05](#m05-phase-averaging-selects-number-sectors).

**Conceptual connection.** The idle rail makes it possible to superpose an empty sensor array with a one-excitation array at the same total excitation.

**Canonical sources.** [Reference-frames bridge](REFERENCE_FRAMES_BRIDGE.md) · [Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610030v3) (§IV A)

### M07 Idle width limits sensor charge

**Central question.** Why do additional idle qubits supply distinct sensor-number values?

**Understanding target.** Show that with $`r`$ idle qubits and fixed total excitation $`Q`$, the sensor-number support lies between $`\max(0,Q-r)`$ and $`\min(N,Q)`$. Count any idle mode with a compensating excitation under the same budget.

**Prerequisites.** [M05](#m05-phase-averaging-selects-number-sectors), [M06](#m06-one-excitation-on-three-rails).

**Conceptual connection.** A width restriction on the allowed spectrum becomes a degree restriction in the converse.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md) · [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M08 Positive effects and dark amplitudes

**Central question.** What does zero probability imply about a positive measurement effect?

**Understanding target.** Use the positive square root of a POVM effect to turn a probability zero into an annihilation condition on a vector. Describe how to complete a set of conclusive effects whose sum is at most identity.

**Prerequisites.** [M03](#m03-conclusive-identification-and-abstention), [M06](#m06-one-excitation-on-three-rails).

**Conceptual connection.** Zero error constrains the subspaces the receiver may use. The abstention effect completes the physical measurement.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md) · [Reference-frames bridge](REFERENCE_FRAMES_BRIDGE.md)

### M09 Parity vectors of sensor subsets

**Central question.** How is a subset of sensors represented before the event?

**Understanding target.** Construct $`Z_A|\psi\rangle`$ by applying one Pauli Z at each of the sites in $`A`$. Interpret the resulting sign as the parity of their occupation.

**Prerequisites.** [M01](#m01-sensor-occupation-and-phase), [M02](#m02-one-interrogation-and-many-possible-subsets).

**Conceptual connection.** Expanding the event product expresses each possible output using these vectors.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md)

### M10 Independent coefficient functions

**Central question.** Why does a fixed measurement over a continuum of angles reveal separate constraints?

**Understanding target.** Factor out a nonzero cosine power on a smaller interval and view the remaining functions as distinct powers of a tangent. Use their independence to separate the vanishing coefficients.

**Prerequisites.** [M03](#m03-conclusive-identification-and-abstention), [M08](#m08-positive-effects-and-dark-amplitudes), [M09](#m09-parity-vectors-of-sensor-subsets).

**Conceptual connection.** This step turns an operational demand across angles into a finite vector-space problem.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md)

### M11 Gram matrices and contractions

**Central question.** How can one detect whether a proposed receiver is feasible without writing its gates?

**Understanding target.** Use the Gram matrix to compute squared norms of arbitrary input-vector combinations. Explain why the output norm must be no greater than the input norm for a proposed linear contraction.

**Prerequisites.** [M08](#m08-positive-effects-and-dark-amplitudes), [M09](#m09-parity-vectors-of-sensor-subsets).

**Conceptual connection.** The contraction construction later turns the maximin objective into a positive-semidefinite matrix condition.

**Canonical sources.** [Finite frontier and scaling](../science/exact-frontier.md)

### M12 The total resource question

**Central question.** Which resources and probabilities have to be compared to claim a saving?

**Understanding target.** Summarize the one-use model, global excitation constraint, idle-qubit budget and zero-error requirement. Work through the matched local single-excitation Bell-pair benchmark and identify what its one idle partner per sensor buys.

**Prerequisites.** [M02](#m02-one-interrogation-and-many-possible-subsets), [M03](#m03-conclusive-identification-and-abstention), [M05](#m05-phase-averaging-selects-number-sectors), [M07](#m07-idle-width-limits-sensor-charge), [M08](#m08-positive-effects-and-dark-amplitudes), [M11](#m11-gram-matrices-and-contractions).

**Conceptual connection.** The benchmark fixes a concrete success rate. The project asks how much of it a smaller shared idle register can recover.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md) · [Receiver implementation](../science/implementation.md) · [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md)

## Phase II — Research narrative

### M13 A wrong label kills each degree

**Central question.** What must the effect for one label do to every other label's orbit?

**Understanding target.** Expand the product of local rotations in the sine and cosine coefficient functions. For each wrong label, identify one forced zero vector sum at every degree.

**Prerequisites.** [M08](#m08-positive-effects-and-dark-amplitudes), [M09](#m09-parity-vectors-of-sensor-subsets), [M10](#m10-independent-coefficient-functions), [M12](#m12-the-total-resource-question).

**Conceptual connection.** The vanishing sums begin the structural reduction from a continuum of output states to a finite parity family.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md)

### M14 Incidence isolates every lower moment

**Central question.** Why can't a conclusive effect use the lower-degree part of its own label?

**Understanding target.** Explain how full column rank and row redundancy of the subset incidence matrix imply that all parity vectors of degree less than the number of hits are killed.

**Prerequisites.** [M09](#m09-parity-vectors-of-sensor-subsets), [M13](#m13-a-wrong-label-kills-each-degree).

**Conceptual connection.** The only surviving term for a conclusive label is the top-degree parity vector of that label.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md)

### M15 The signal factor and receiver gain

**Central question.** Where does the dependence on the unknown rotation angle enter success?

**Understanding target.** Separate the exact factor $`\sin^{2k}(\theta/2)`$ from the angle-independent receiver gain. Explain why an interval approaching the identity can have zero worst-angle success despite a positive gain.

**Prerequisites.** [M03](#m03-conclusive-identification-and-abstention), [M13](#m13-a-wrong-label-kills-each-degree), [M14](#m14-incidence-isolates-every-lower-moment).

**Conceptual connection.** The rest of the analysis optimizes the gain, keeping the signal weakness explicit.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md) · [Finite frontier and scaling](../science/exact-frontier.md)

### M16 The summed parity becomes a polynomial

**Central question.** How can one vector control the sum of the conclusive gains?

**Understanding target.** Show that the sum of size-$`k`$ parity observables depends only on the sensor excitation and is a degree-$`k`$ polynomial. Subtract lower-degree polynomials without changing any conclusive amplitude.

**Prerequisites.** [M07](#m07-idle-width-limits-sensor-charge), [M09](#m09-parity-vectors-of-sensor-subsets), [M14](#m14-incidence-isolates-every-lower-moment), [M15](#m15-the-signal-factor-and-receiver-gain).

**Conceptual connection.** A single polynomial residual on the allowed charges bounds the combined gain over all labels.

**Canonical sources.** [Operational model](../science/model.md) · [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M17 Too few idle qubits

**Central question.** Why can every conclusive effect be forced to vanish on its own state?

**Understanding target.** Interpolate the top-degree charge polynomial by a lower-degree polynomial when there are at most $`k`$ sensor charge values. Apply the residual bound to get zero gain for all subsets.

**Prerequisites.** [M07](#m07-idle-width-limits-sensor-charge), [M16](#m16-the-summed-parity-becomes-a-polynomial).

**Conceptual connection.** This proves the onset is at least $`k`$ idle qubits, even for global readout and invariant mixed probes.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M18 The upper bound at the first feasible width

**Central question.** Why is the gain initially suppressed by the number of candidate subsets?

**Understanding target.** Follow the alternating-sign choice of residual on $`k+1`$ consecutive charge values. Use the sum bound to get a worst-subset gain of at most $`1/\binom Nk`$.

**Prerequisites.** [M16](#m16-the-summed-parity-becomes-a-polynomial), [M17](#m17-too-few-idle-qubits).

**Conceptual connection.** At the onset, one has enough charge support to identify subsets but still faces the sum constraint across all subset labels.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M19 A probe spanning the required charge values

**Central question.** What probe makes the onset bound attainable?

**Understanding target.** Build one charge-tagged state across the sensor strings of weights zero through $`k`$, assigning a binomial weight to each layer and uniform probabilities within that layer.

**Prerequisites.** [M06](#m06-one-excitation-on-three-rails), [M07](#m07-idle-width-limits-sensor-charge), [M18](#m18-the-upper-bound-at-the-first-feasible-width).

**Conceptual connection.** The probe preserves just enough charge variation within one total sector to support a different conclusive direction for each subset.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M20 The reciprocal receiver at the onset

**Central question.** How can one isolate one top-degree parity vector from all the others?

**Understanding target.** Trace the signed sum over the subsets of one label. Explain how finite differences cancel all other parity contributions and select that label.

**Prerequisites.** [M08](#m08-positive-effects-and-dark-amplitudes), [M09](#m09-parity-vectors-of-sensor-subsets), [M14](#m14-incidence-isolates-every-lower-moment), [M19](#m19-a-probe-spanning-the-required-charge-values).

**Conceptual connection.** These reciprocal vectors are explicit measurement directions for the state prepared in the previous milestone.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M21 Attaining the exact threshold

**Central question.** How are the reciprocal directions made into a complete measurement?

**Understanding target.** Use the nonnegative reciprocal Gram entries and their common row sum to normalize the conclusive effects, then add the abstention effect.

**Prerequisites.** [M11](#m11-gram-matrices-and-contractions), [M18](#m18-the-upper-bound-at-the-first-feasible-width), [M20](#m20-the-reciprocal-receiver-at-the-onset).

**Conceptual connection.** The resulting worst-label gain matches the upper bound for every subset, closing both feasibility and performance at the onset.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M22 One-hit gain from number variance

**Central question.** Why does a problem with a single hit depend on the variance of sensor excitation?

**Understanding target.** Derive the upper bound by subtracting the mean number from the degree-one radial sum. Use the width of the number range to bound the variance.

**Prerequisites.** [M07](#m07-idle-width-limits-sensor-charge), [M15](#m15-the-signal-factor-and-receiver-gain), [M16](#m16-the-summed-parity-becomes-a-polynomial).

**Conceptual connection.** This gives a bound proportional to the square of the idle width per sensor, independently of the circuit.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md)

### M23 One-hit probes from charge moments

**Central question.** How can a symmetric distribution attain the variance limit?

**Understanding target.** Read the radial and angular eigenvalues of the one-hit covariance Gram matrix. Explain how centered endpoints serve below saturation and moment matching serves at or above saturation.

**Prerequisites.** [M11](#m11-gram-matrices-and-contractions), [M19](#m19-a-probe-spanning-the-required-charge-values), [M22](#m22-one-hit-gain-from-number-variance).

**Conceptual connection.** The radial and angular components expose which part of the collective receiver limits its gain.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md) · [Receiver implementation](../science/implementation.md)

### M24 The exact one-hit resource curve

**Central question.** When can a shared idle register recover the local receiver's success?

**Understanding target.** Connect the converse and the attaining distributions to gain $`\min\{1,r^2/N\}`$. Interpret the integer saturation width $`\lceil\sqrt N\rceil`$ alongside the original $`N`$ idle partners.

**Prerequisites.** [M12](#m12-the-total-resource-question), [M21](#m21-attaining-the-exact-threshold), [M22](#m22-one-hit-gain-from-number-variance), [M23](#m23-one-hit-probes-from-charge-moments).

**Conceptual connection.** This is an exact comparison of idle-qubit budgets at equal conclusive probability. Circuit cost is a further quantity.

**Canonical sources.** [Exact endpoint theorems](../science/exact-endpoints.md) · [Receiver implementation](../science/implementation.md) · [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md)

### M25 An upper bound for intermediate widths

**Central question.** How does the polynomial residual constrain the many-hit frontier?

**Understanding target.** Use a rescaled Chebyshev polynomial with the required leading coefficient to control the residual uniformly on the charge interval. Read off the power of idle width and the normalization by the number of labels.

**Prerequisites.** [M15](#m15-the-signal-factor-and-receiver-gain), [M16](#m16-the-summed-parity-becomes-a-polynomial), [M18](#m18-the-upper-bound-at-the-first-feasible-width).

**Conceptual connection.** The bound predicts how large a reference budget is needed for fixed nonvanishing gain at fixed hit count.

**Canonical sources.** [Finite frontier and scaling](../science/exact-frontier.md)

### M26 A modular receiver from balanced partitions

**Central question.** How can many-hit sensing reuse a one-hit design without making wrong reports?

**Understanding target.** Split the sensors randomly into balanced groups and run a one-hit receiver in each. Show why a bad split has an empty group that necessarily abstains, and how a good split contributes a product of gains.

**Prerequisites.** [M03](#m03-conclusive-identification-and-abstention), [M23](#m23-one-hit-probes-from-charge-moments), [M24](#m24-the-exact-one-hit-resource-curve), [M25](#m25-an-upper-bound-for-intermediate-widths).

**Conceptual connection.** Randomization makes the splitting probability independent of the true subset and yields a constructive match to the upper bound in order.

**Canonical sources.** [Finite frontier and scaling](../science/exact-frontier.md) · [Receiver implementation](../science/implementation.md)

### M27 The exact finite optimization

**Central question.** How can the best gain be computed for arbitrary finite parameters?

**Understanding target.** Identify the finite charge supports, build the parity moment Gram matrix, and impose the contraction inequality for uniform top-degree outputs. Explain why symmetrizing the occupation distribution loses no worst-label gain.

**Prerequisites.** [M07](#m07-idle-width-limits-sensor-charge), [M11](#m11-gram-matrices-and-contractions), [M14](#m14-incidence-isolates-every-lower-moment), [M15](#m15-the-signal-factor-and-receiver-gain), [M23](#m23-one-hit-probes-from-charge-moments).

**Conceptual connection.** The same charge-dependent data specifies both a numerical optimization and a physical measurement construction.

**Canonical sources.** [Finite frontier and scaling](../science/exact-frontier.md)

### M28 Fixed-hit scaling and full gain

**Central question.** What does square-root reference width buy when the hit count stays fixed?

**Understanding target.** Combine the width obstruction and the modular protocol to obtain the fixed-hit scaling. Explain how reproducing binomial low-degree parity moments can make the Gram directions orthogonal and reach full normalized gain.

**Prerequisites.** [M15](#m15-the-signal-factor-and-receiver-gain), [M25](#m25-an-upper-bound-for-intermediate-widths), [M26](#m26-a-modular-receiver-from-balanced-partitions), [M27](#m27-the-exact-finite-optimization).

**Conceptual connection.** Distinguish the width needed for first nonzero success, for a constant fraction of the benchmark, and for exact saturation.

**Canonical sources.** [Finite frontier and scaling](../science/exact-frontier.md)

### M29 Operating the collective receiver

**Central question.** Which operations turn the measurement prescription into an excitation-conserving circuit?

**Understanding target.** Locate the sector preparation, measurement dilation and neutral output record in the implementation. Distinguish idle qubits used during the event from gate count, connectivity and post-event output storage.

**Prerequisites.** [M06](#m06-one-excitation-on-three-rails), [M12](#m12-the-total-resource-question), [M20](#m20-the-reciprocal-receiver-at-the-onset), [M24](#m24-the-exact-one-hit-resource-curve), [M26](#m26-a-modular-receiver-from-balanced-partitions), [M27](#m27-the-exact-finite-optimization).

**Conceptual connection.** The collective design saves charged bypass qubits compared with local Bell pairs, while its circuit can require more shared control.

**Canonical sources.** [Receiver implementation](../science/implementation.md) · [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md)

### M30 The complete receiver story

**Central question.** How do physical symmetry, zero-error discrimination and the resource law fit together?

**Understanding target.** Explain why the no-error promise isolates top-degree parity, how global number conservation bounds the available charge support, and how the onset receiver, one-hit formula and finite frontier solve the resulting design problem.

**Prerequisites.** [M15](#m15-the-signal-factor-and-receiver-gain), [M21](#m21-attaining-the-exact-threshold), [M24](#m24-the-exact-one-hit-resource-curve), [M28](#m28-fixed-hit-scaling-and-full-gain), [M29](#m29-operating-the-collective-receiver).

**Conceptual connection.** State the regime and resource metric when comparing the result with known angle or uncapped bypass schemes. Distinguish the analytical conclusions from finite numerical and circuit checks.

**Canonical sources.** [Scientific account](../science/README.md) · [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md) · [Receiver implementation](../science/implementation.md)
