# From a phase reference to a trajectory receiver

Suppose two qubit sensors see the same event window and exactly one is rotated about its $`Z`$ axis. The rotation angle is unknown. A receiver needs a way to distinguish the two sites without comparing either qubit with an external phase standard. The simple option is to let the sensing qubits share an excitation with an idle qubit.

## Educational anchor

Stephen D. Bartlett, Terry Rudolph and Robert W. Spekkens, *Reference frames, superselection rules, and quantum information*, Reviews of Modern Physics **79**, 555–609 (2007). Read [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610030v3), Sections II B–C on phase averaging and symmetry restrictions, then Section IV A on relational descriptions of phase. The [published review](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.79.555) is also available.

| Anchor section | What to carry into this project |
| --- | --- |
| II B | An unknown common phase is represented by averaging over the transformations it generates. This removes coherence between different values of total number. |
| II C | The same construction works for other symmetries. For this project it gives block-diagonal states and measurement effects under the group generated by total excitation. |
| IV A | Phase information can live in the relative amplitudes of two registers that together have definite total number. The idle register can store a compensating excitation. |

The review's mode number becomes the qubit excitation observable. The sensor register has number $`W`$, the idle register has number $`B`$, and the common phase acts through $`e^{i\varphi(W+B)}`$. Within a sector of total number $`Q`$, different sensor numbers remain coherent when the idle qubits carry the corresponding $`Q-W`$ excitations. The unknown event angle $`\theta`$ is a separate parameter. It acts only on the hit sensors and can change relative phases within a total-number sector.

## A complete two-sensor readout

Use one excitation spread over three qubits. Write $`|b\rangle`$ for occupation of the idle qubit, and $`|1\rangle,|2\rangle`$ for occupation of the two sensor qubits. Prepare

```math
|\psi\rangle=\frac{|b\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}+
\frac{|1\rangle+|2\rangle}{2}.
```

All three basis states have total number one, so their relative amplitudes survive the unknown common phase. If sensor $`i`$ is hit, the event multiplies just $`|i\rangle`$ by $`e^{i\theta}`$ after removing an overall phase. The new state is

```math
|\psi_i(\theta)\rangle =
|\psi\rangle+\frac{e^{i\theta}-1}{2}|i\rangle.
```

For each site use a measurement effect $`E_i=|f_i\rangle\langle f_i|`$, with $`|f_i\rangle=|i\rangle/\sqrt{2}-|b\rangle/2`$. Its amplitude on the unrotated state is zero, and it is orthogonal to $`|j\rangle`$ when $`j\ne i`$. The wrong effect therefore has probability zero for every angle. The two effects sum to an operator with largest eigenvalue one. Indeed, their vectors have squared norms $`{3}/{4}`$ and mutual overlap $`{1}/{4}`$. The rest of the identity is the abstention effect. The correct effect gives

```math
\langle\psi_i(\theta)|E_i|\psi_i(\theta)\rangle
=\frac{|e^{i\theta}-1|^2}{8}
=\frac12\sin^2(\theta/2).
```

The example shows the two parts of the receiver design. Relational coherence keeps the phase shift observable without a shared phase standard. The measurement gives a correct site label whenever its outcome is conclusive, without needing the angle to choose its settings. At angles for which the physical rotation is the identity up to phase, it always abstains.

## From the example to the general result

The [model](../science/model.md) extends the amplitude cancellation to all candidate subsets. Requiring zero error throughout an open interval of angles forces the wrong effects to kill entire families of parity-weighted vectors. The [exact endpoints](../science/exact-endpoints.md) use this constraint to derive the idle-width threshold and build the measurement that reaches it. The [finite frontier](../science/exact-frontier.md) turns the same cancellation into an optimization over charge distributions.
